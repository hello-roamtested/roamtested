## I Accidentally Bought a China eSIM on Trip.com

I wasn't trying to buy an eSIM for China. I was booking a flight to China on <Aff provider="en/tripcom">Trip.com</Aff>, then I noticed the site also sold eSIMs — 500MB a day for $0.10. I've bought enough flights here to trust the site, and at that price the downside was basically zero.

So I bought it, flew to Chengdu, and tested it for real.

## Plans and pricing
Trip.com offers six eSIM plans for China, grouped by region — mainland China, Hong Kong, and Taiwan — as well as app accessibility. Each plan is highly flexible: you can choose from 1 to 30 days and select either a daily data allowance or a total data package. Bookings are renewable and come with free cancellation up to the day before activation. 

This flexibility makes it easy to match different travel needs. 

## Buying and activation
To buy it, simply search for “eSIM for China” on Trip.com, choose the plan you need, and complete your purchase. To activate it, the confirmation page displays a simple One Tap button. Tap it, follow the prompts, and the eSIM installs and activates automatically. A QR code is also available if you prefer the manual setup method, though I didn’t need it.

From start to finish, it takes about 60 seconds. 

## Using it day to day
I tested every plan, and for daily use I bought the 500 MB/day option. I used it on an iPhone 12 mini the way most travelers would — while walking through the airport, riding the metro, staying in hotels, and moving through crowded malls at peak hours.

On the daily plan, I recorded speeds of 82.9 Mbps download and 51.3 Mbps upload. It also worked well with most of the everyday apps travelers need.

The one plan to avoid — the "All AI" plan. This is my biggest warning. When I tested it, the connection dropped to LTE and became noticeably slower. It was not a coverage issue, because at the same time and in the same location, the other plans were still delivering normal network performance. Same phone, same spot, almost the same time — but a much worse result. That suggests the issue is with the plan itself, not the location. For that reason, I can’t recommend it.

## Who should buy it — and who shouldn't
**Buy it if you:**
- Are a short-term traveler to China and want Google, Instagram, Maps, and AI apps to work without setting up a VPN.
- Want the cheapest realistic option — 500MB a day for $0.10 is about as low as eSIM pricing gets.
- Value flexible plans — you can match days and data almost exactly to your trip instead of overpaying for a fixed bundle.

**Skip it if you:**
- Mainly need very fast internet for heavy tasks like streaming in 4K, uploading large files, or sharing the connection with multiple users.
